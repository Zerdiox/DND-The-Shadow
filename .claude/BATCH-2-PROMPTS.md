## 2. Heartseeker Dagger

Armoury chest 6, `magical: true`. **UNSEEN: the gem.** The user asked for
"a red gem shaped roughly like an anatomically correct heart"; deep garnet is
this campaign's colour for menace, and the pommel is where ornament belongs
on a gripped weapon.

```
Create an image of a finely made dagger lying flat, about 30 cm overall,
built from the pommel up: a plain steel disc pommel set with a deep garnet-red
gem carved as an anatomically correct heart, its chambers and great vessels
shaped into the stone; a grip of thick dark leather wrapped tight over the
tang for about 11 cm, one closed hand's width; a short straight steel
crossguard; and a double-edged steel blade about 18 cm long tapering to a fine
point. The steel is bright and crisply ground, the leather dark and close
fitted.

The object itself is pristine and undimmed — no corrosion, no tarnish, no
wear, crisp sharp detail, in contrast with the aged surface it rests on.

Style: a single object from a high-fantasy world, made by pre-modern hands in
the tradition of a long-lost classical civilisation — hand-forged, cast and
carved. Painted as a fantasy roleplaying rulebook item illustration: a digital
painting with visible brushwork, confident edges and painterly texture
throughout. The object alone and centred, filling about two thirds of the
frame, resting on the iron lid of a chest in a stripped armoury. Low raking
lamplight from the upper left, one soft shadow beneath, spreading to the lower
right. The setting fills the frame edge to edge with no border or margin, held
shallow and quiet so the object stays the subject. Restrained, desaturated
colour throughout, the setting keeping whatever hue it naturally has. A clean,
unlettered plate.
```

The heartbeat sense is left out: it is felt, not seen.

## 7. Vantablack Coin

Vault chest 1, `magical: unknown`. Small, so bare stone.
**UNSEEN: the condition line.** A coin that absorbs light cannot show tarnish
without contradicting itself, so the wear goes to its edge.

```
Create an image of a coin about 3 cm across lying flat, its face a completely
flat matte black that gives back no highlight at all, so that it reads as a
hole cut in the plate rather than as an object, with only a thin line of
lamplight along its milled rim to show it has thickness.

The object is worn as found — its rim rubbed smooth with handling and age,
its face unmarked.

Style: [the fixed block, resting on a flat slab of weathered grey-green stone,
the chamber floor, dry and faintly pitted with fine hairline cracks]
```

The blackness is stated as what the face *does* — gives back no highlight,
reads as a hole — rather than as "do not add shine", which would invite
shine.

## 9. Clay Mapmaker

Astral Chamber, `magical: true`. **UNSEEN: the dish form.** The note says only
"a clay construction"; the user said they would read it as a dish shape.

The Astral Chamber is one of the four rooms that earns a mark on the object,
so the chalky spotting clause is included.

```
Create an image of a shallow clay dish about 25 cm across, hand-thrown from
pale grey-brown clay, with a broad flat floor and a low rounded rim, its inner
floor smooth and empty.

The object itself is pristine and undimmed — no corrosion, no tarnish, no
wear, crisp sharp detail, in contrast with the aged surface it rests on.

Faint chalky white spotting where liquid dripped onto it over a long time.

Style: [the fixed block, resting on a stone table under long-overflowed
flasks]
```

**One thing to settle with the user first.** The note says it was found
smashed and repaired with *mending*, which in the rules leaves no trace — so
a pristine, seamless dish is the correct reading. Faint repair lines would
tell the story better but would contradict the spell. Ask which they want
before sending; do not decide it alone.
