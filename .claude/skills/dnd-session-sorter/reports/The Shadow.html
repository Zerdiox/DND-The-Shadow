Session 07 · 6 November 2026

The Wizard's Harvest

A mercy that didn't take, the last loop of the Cradle walked and the observatory never found — and then a wizard standing on top of our dam, explaining his plans to the five people best placed to stop him.

The thing on the chains

We went back to help it

We had left it hanging once already. On the way up we stopped to see whether anything could be done, and the answer came back quickly: no.

Muradin put a mud grenade into the chains and they gave way. It came down hard, snapped off some of its own spikes on the floor, and lay there covered in mud in exactly as much pain as before. Freeing it had changed nothing.

Aslaug dug deep and said the thing out loud that the rest of us had been circling. It is not made for this plane. There was nothing here that could help it.

So Muradin took his warhammer to it, and it burst.

What was left deflated into a mess on the floor. We filled three bottles with its blood before we went — the same white fluid that had been dripping into those overflowed flasks for longer than anyone has been alive, and the thing the builders used to open their way into the Astral Plane.

The way out

Both cylinders, and a hole in the ceiling

The great hall had two bronze cylinders and we had always assumed two ways down.

The Butterfly Cave

↓ the left cylinder

The stair runs down through rock and then out into an open cave, caged in metal the whole way — which tells you something about what the builders expected to meet here.

Black butterflies drift in the open air beyond the cage. They don't look like dark butterflies. They look like holes punched in the world. We left them alone and kept walking.

The Glass Floor Room

↓ to the bottom

The floor is glass, and through it we were looking down into the poison cage — the green ball in its iron bars that cost Vicaster twenty-two points and a familiar, three sessions ago. We had squeezed past it at floor level. Now we were standing on top of it.

The stair at the far end went back up, and came out in the other cylinder. Two ways down, and they are the same way. There is no third.

The long hall

↑ through the armoury

Out past the empty weapon racks and up a long staircase to a small room, with a long hall running off it. Drivik went ahead checking for traps and found nothing. The hall simply ended.

Then he looked up. Thirty or forty feet of shaft in the ceiling, and a bronze mouth at the top of it.

The Inverted Pyramid

↑ out

Vicaster put fly on Drivik, Muradin and himself. Muradin began being sick the instant his boots left the floor and did not stop, because dwarves do not do flying. Echo went up as an owl. Aslaug was carried.

The mouth was a trap door. It opened among the statues, and we climbed out into the pyramid we first walked into five sessions ago.

Out of the Cradle — and the observatory still unfound.

Above ground

Two answers on the road home

Vicaster cast contact other plane and asked Aurorion who could help us with the Shadow. The answer was Orebridge. Not a ruin, not a vault, not somewhere a thousand feet down. Our own home town, where we could have asked at any point in the last seven sessions.

Then we walked back to the dam, and Rem Koolhuis was standing on top of it.

He is the wizard who ferried us here in the first place, before any of this was written down. He did not deny anything or dress it up. He is taking the dam: hold it through the winter, let the melt drive the three golems already standing in front of it, and harvest magical power with them.

Nobody drew a weapon. We went home.

Orebridge

Home, and doing better than we are

  • Passed Castle on the way. Thriving.
  • Orebridge has flourished — trees in bloom, fields worked, the dwarven invasion long since undone by the grove.
  • Reported everything to Theodin and lent him the disease and poison books — five volumes, both halves of the Shadow among them.
  • He pointed us at the druids, who have been working on the plague for years, and came with us.
  • They need more time. So we are taking an extended break.

Session 06 · 23 October 2026

The Silver Tongue

A puddle with three bad ideas in it, a dying thing on a chain that turns out to be a piece of machinery, and — on a shelf, unremarked — both halves of the thing we came down here to stop.

The descent

Down the ladder from the great hall

The Puddle

→ tunnel, halfway down

A tunnel opened off the ladder onto a large puddle, and under the water lay three things. Detect magic lit up exactly one of them. We worked out what the other two did the hard way.

Afterwards we noticed the water was oily rather than wet, and Drivik — who had tasted it, because of course he had — reported it was very salty. We left the sword and the baby where they were. The tongue came with us, wrapped in canvas.

The Astral Chamber

↓ ladder

A square room, thirty by fifty, with something hanging from the ceiling on chains: a crystalline structure full of spikes, moving, alive. White drops fall from it into flasks that overflowed a very long time ago.

Aslaug knew what it was. A being of the Astral Plane, the kind that is as near immortal as makes no difference — and this one is close to the end. The white liquid is its blood.

On a table nearby lay a smashed clay construction. Vicaster mended it and found he was holding something that draws you the plan of a building or a perfect map of an area. Muradin opened the far door with a little guidance, and we went down again.

The Alchemy Hall

↓ stairs

Benches, desks and alcoves of alchemical equipment, every bit of it dried out and gone gooey. One cupboard held logbooks and diaries. Another held nineteen unlabelled vials of coloured liquid, all sealed — and under detect magic, all nineteen lit up.

The shelves were project books with code names like Angelic Pig, next to volumes on planar travel, magic for military fabrication, and one on cursing fabric and clothes. Three books on poisons. Two on diseases. Nothing at all on undead.

One of them explained the room upstairs. The builders had found a way into the Astral Plane — a portal opened with the blood of the thing hanging in the chains — and our reading ties it to the poison portal we already found. It was never a specimen. It was a component.

The two disease books mattered more. One covers a hive mind. The other covers plagues, and carries a handwritten note about the unforeseen results of the two coming into contact. We know what those results are.

This is where the Shadow was written down.

Neither book holds a remedy. Between them they are still the best lead we have.

The Sealed Room

→ the metal door

A giant metal door stood at the far end of the hall. Drivik knocked on it. Something knocked back.

Muradin opened it anyway. The room decompressed into ours with a bang and a cloud of metallic, acidic air, and left an oily sheen on everyone's skin. What came out behind the gas was enormous, fleshy, twisted and poison green — a microbe grown to the size of a room.

It was a hard fight. We won it. The long rest afterwards took all of us to level 9.

Session 05 · 16 October 2026

This Is My Book

Something boneless and white came up the chain, three spells did nothing to it, and so we went down instead — into a room where a dead woman's soul was still guarding her book.

The thing in the shaft

What is coming up the chain

Drivik caught it at the very edge of his darkvision, a hundred and twenty feet down, and went back for a longer look: something pale and white, slowly climbing the chain. It takes up the entire shaft.

The closer it came, the clearer it got. A giant, compacted into the space it was climbing through, hauling itself up with its one free hand. Its body moves like a liquid blob with no bones in it at all. It can reach an arm a long way down a ladder after something it wants.

It is still alive, and when it could no longer follow us down, it turned around and started climbing back up.

The descent

Trading levels with it

Every time we checked, it had gained another twenty feet.

The Strata Gallery

↑ back up

We came back up to the blocks, and that was where Drivik spotted it. Three spells later it was obvious we weren't going to win this by shooting at it, so the plan became going around and below.

The Crystal Hall

→ across the shaft

Back across into the salt chamber — and the sisters weren't there. Their sister healed and her heart back where it belonged, the six of them had returned to their own plane. We went down.

The Shell Room

↓ ladder

Muradin went first into a room full of shells — clams, spirals, crab shells, dried coral, every one of them empty. It reads like a piece of sea floor left indoors. No ladder down from here, but a tunnel on the far side led towards a stack of crates.

The Art Store

→ across the shaft

Drivik jumped first and handed everyone else over. No traps. The first wooden chest held a sculpture — a woman in a toga with one hand outstretched, nearly as tall as Drivik. We opened a few more and found the same thing everywhere: the whole store is art, crated and waiting. We left it there.

The Floorboard Cave

↓ ladder

A cave stacked with floorboards, cut and ready to lay. We checked the shaft from here. Another twenty feet gained.

The Tool Storage

→ across the shaft

A tiled hallway opening into a bigger room: hammers, chisels, the ordinary contents of a workshop — and among them a few implements Drivik couldn't place at all, however long he looked. There was a ladder down, which by then was the only thing that mattered. The giant reached our level and went into the room with the wood, coming slowly for Aslaug, Echo and Vicaster.

The Spider Chamber

↓ ladder

Vicaster held it off while the others climbed, then followed. The round chamber below had hieroglyphs and drawings across its walls, and in the middle something like a spider, curled up on its back. Held in its legs was a block of ice with a woman inside it.

The shaft looked empty. Then an arm came down the ladder, closed around Vicaster, and lifted him up to be eaten — until Aslaug's command made it let go.

Vicaster put a hand on the ice. The cold reminded him of the wraiths back home in the library, though the magic wasn't coming from the woman and wasn't wraith magic held in check either. Then he cut the block open, and her soul came out of it screaming.

STOP, THIS IS MY BOOK.

We beat it, and took the book off her body.

The Stables

↓ stairs

Muradin had already seen what was below: stalls built for horses, and the shapes of boats across the far side. Drivik went down and found a great door leading away from the shaft. Above us the giant, unable to follow any further, turned around and began climbing.

The Great Hall

→ the bridge, 90 ft

The bridge runs ninety feet out from the stables across open darkness, and ends in an enormous hall with nothing in it but two bronze cylinders. Each has a door, and inside each door a stair going down. We didn't take them.

The Armoury

↑ two ladders

Up instead: a ladder to a shelf in the cave wall, then a second ladder into another hall. A weapon store, long since stripped — the racks are bare. But ten iron chests still stand against the wall, locked and untrapped, and Drivik picked every one of them.

The descent

Down the shaft

The Shaft

the handle · the chain

Rested and a good deal richer, we went back to the handle. Muradin put his shoulder into it and got nowhere — an athletics 13 was never going to do it alone — but with Drivik pushing too it finally gave, and somewhere further into the room the chain began to move. That was all. Vicaster tried a different approach: he cast light on a stone and dropped it down the hole, and we watched it shrink to a pinprick until there was nothing left to see.

The Weighing Station

↓ stairs

So we took the stairs instead. They run alongside the shaft, down past a platform hung on chains — a weighing system for ore, its tubs long empty — and keep going until they bottom out in a cave. From there an open door looks straight onto the shaft: the chain hanging in the middle, and on the far side of the gap, another opening.

The Cervit Storage

→ across the gap

There was nothing for it but to jump. Drivik went first and made it look easy with a 22; Vicaster, Muradin and Aslaug followed with a 20, a 21 and a showy 26. Echo rolled a 5 — and arrived as a raven, having wild shaped somewhere on the way down.

On the other side was a storeroom of rusted, rotting crates, though a few had held up. Inside those were strips of a pale, resin-like material, and Drivik's natural 20 put a name to it: Cervit, an alloy people still turn up now and then but nobody knows how to make any more. By the time the crates were empty we had around ninety kilos of it, white and black, and we split it between us.

The Orb Room

↑ ladder

A ladder led up into a room of sturdy metal tables, with a broken half-dome of panels hanging from the ceiling. Along one wall, four crystal orbs sat on pedestals, each a third bigger than a football and full of strange, mutated underground plants. Our skin prickled as we came close. So did Drivik's tongue, because of course he licked one. Detect magic showed nothing, Vicaster's arcana could only offer “alchemy,” and the machine overhead hung just too high for Muradin to get a proper look at.

The Fabric Room

↓ down the chain

From there Drivik simply stepped onto the chain and slid down it, trusting to Reliable Talent, into a room buried in heaps of old fabric. The rest of us climbed down after him, more carefully. Further in, a ladder dropped away again, and below it something was glowing a faint green.

We would come back through here later and find out what the heaps were hiding: spectres wrapped in chains, rising out of the cloth. It was a short fight, and once beaten they fled.

The Poison Cage

↓ ladder

The green came from a glowing ball inside a metal cage. Vicaster sent his familiar through the bars in sphinx of wonder form, and it died, painfully. He summoned it back and gave it candy, which seemed only fair. Getting close himself cost him 22 poison damage, and when Drivik put an arrow into the ball, the arrow simply vanished.

Vicaster's arcana supplied the rest: this was a portal to the para-elemental plane of poison. We gave it a very wide berth on the way past.

The Crystal Hall

↓ ladder, 20 ft

Twenty feet further down, the ladder opened onto a room made of crystal, spirals carved into its floor. Six women of crystal lounged on platforms of salt — Drivik would later lick one to be sure — with spikes growing from their bodies and broken gemstones where their eyes should be. Drivik took one look and scrambled back up.

The familiar went down in his place and asked how they were doing. They seemed amused, and allowed that we might not be a treat if we minded our manners. So Vicaster went down first, with a bow, and the rest of us followed.

They spoke one at a time, each sister taking her turn. They had been brought here against their will, from a place where drought and purification rule, and had stayed this long — though time, they said, is relative — because one of them had had her heart stolen. That heart turned out to be the milky crystal from chest 19 of the vault. We gave it back, they healed their sister, and then they told us where we were.

We are in the Cradle.

The place we had been looking for all along. A facility built to study deadly and dangerous things, they said, with an observatory at its very bottom where the builders searched for whatever their research needed.

Session 03 · 4 September 2026

The Hungry Ambassador

A crown that frightens everyone but its wearer, a glass statue that waves back, and something in the dark below that calls itself an ambassador right up until it gets hungry.

The descent

Five rooms, one way down

The Throne Room

throne · mace · crown

An ornamental throne, mace and crown beneath a serpent relief nobody could place — Aslaug's history check turned up nothing. Drivik found no traps, then read the room with arcana: divination on the throne, enchantment on the crown, and both at once on the mace.

Muradin took the mace. Vicaster took the crown and put it on, which cost everyone else a wisdom save and left the party standing in fear until he removed it. Echo then sat on the throne and became the only thing anyone could look at. Drivik gave him treats.

The Glass Statue Room

tubes · aquariums · a portal

A room built as a network of glass tubing and aquariums. At its centre stood a glass statue of an old wise man — fancy clothes, serene expression, a glass pipe running down into its head.

As Drivik came up the stairs, liquid began moving into the statue. It filled, it moved, and it waved at him. Then it pointed at the portal at the far end of the room.

The Glass Dome

first contact

Vicaster's familiar went through first, into a round chamber with a single door and, beyond it, a passage of segments narrowing until only one person could pass. The liquid left the statue, spread through the dome, and introduced itself: Zeernebooch, ambassador.

It spoke of the Deep Kingdom — deeper than any person can reach. The builders of this complex had been studying the ground, it said, and made a magical mechanism to bring things from far below back up. The narrowing chambers were a contraption built so its people could talk to ours at all.

Then the door closed, and it said it was hungry.

Spellcasting from Vicaster and a gorilla from Echo got the party out.

The Library

geology · four metal scrolls

Shelves of books, almost all of them about geology and the study of the ground. The find was four scrolls, in two titles: Addictions of Abyssal Gods and Narcotics and Vices of the Creators. They name gods nobody at the table has heard of — the working theory is former pretenders.

The more interesting thing may be the scrolls themselves: made of a metal-like material, very sturdy, and sharp.

The Vault

twenty chests

Up the stairs from the library: twenty large chests, some open, some closed. The party opened all twenty and came away with nineteen finds, catalogued below. Then they retreated to the main room with the deep hole.

Session 02 · 29 August 2026

The Pier and the Tower

Out of the dam, along a cracking river, and down into an inverted pyramid under a tower at the end of a pier.

The river

out of the dam

A giant bird lay across the river with its wings making a bridge, being eaten by eels as it lay there. We healed it, and it took off before anyone could cast speak with animals. Downstream the ground cracked under us, scattered with the corpses of small crustaceans and black tubes venting green smoke.

The Pier and the Tower

→ downstream

We reached a pier with a tower at the end of it, crashed the pier, and explored the tower. Hidden in its floor was a hole, with mirrored bunkers at the four corners and a great quantity of black tubing.

The Inverted Pyramid

↓ the hole

The hole is an inverted pyramid in big steps, lined with rows of naked Roman calcite statues, each on its own level. Below that: a room with a forty-foot metal wheel, a handle joined to it by one enormous piece of leather, and a chain running down into the dark. Observation rooms looked down through transparent floors, and in one of them we found the prismatic keys.

Key decisions

  • Healed the giant bird.
  • Explored the tower and the inverted pyramid.
  • Decided not to touch the throne, crown and mace.

Notable moments

  • Finding the prismatic keys — still the one thing nobody has identified.
  • Discovering the inverted pyramid beneath the tower.

Session 01 · 1 August 2026

The Dam

Six dead Lemurian engineers, thirteen iron pots sealed with magic, and a mummy who would not talk about the cradle.

The Dam

six sarcophagi

The dam holds six sarcophagi containing the remains of six Lemurian engineers, all of them dead — among them Gaius Seberam, Octanius Rex and Quintus Valerian. Beyond the sarcophagi lay a room of thirteen iron pots sealed with magic: necromancy, transmutation, enchantment. Statues stood about with plates showing symbols nobody could read.

The tomb

the ship sarcophagus

In the tomb was a ship sarcophagus with a half-mummified man hanging out of it, mummified dogs alongside: Marius Caldus, a subject of the emperor who helped build the dam. Speak with dead got us the highlights of his life, a warning that the giant glass ball on the model is a trap — and a refusal.

He would not say anything about the cradle.

Key decisions

  • Investigated the tomb and talked to the mummy.
  • Advanced on the strange figures in the tomb.

Notable moments

  • Confronting Marius Caldus.
  • Finding the magical traps and mechanisms built into the dam.
  • The first mention of the cradle — which took four more sessions to reach.
