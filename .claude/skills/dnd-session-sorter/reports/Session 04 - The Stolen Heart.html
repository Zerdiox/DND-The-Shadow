The descent

Down the shaft

The Shaft

the handle · the chain

Rested and a good deal richer, we went back to the handle. Muradin put his shoulder into it and got nowhere — an athletics 13 was never going to do it alone — but with Drivik pushing too it finally gave, and somewhere further into the room the chain began to move. That was all. Vicaster tried a different approach: he cast light on a stone and dropped it down the hole, and we watched it shrink to a pinprick until there was nothing left to see.

The Weighing Station

↓ stairs

So we took the stairs instead. They run alongside the shaft, down past a platform hung on chains — a weighing system for ore, its tubs long empty — and keep going until they bottom out in a cave. From there an open door looks straight onto the shaft: the chain hanging in the middle, and on the far side of the gap, another opening.

The Cervit Storage

→ across the gap

There was nothing for it but to jump. Drivik went first and made it look easy with a 22; Vicaster, Muradin and Aslaug followed with a 20, a 21 and a showy 26. Echo rolled a 5 — and arrived as a raven, having wild shaped somewhere on the way down.

On the other side was a storeroom of rusted, rotting crates, though a few had held up. Inside those were strips of a pale, resin-like material, and Drivik's natural 20 put a name to it: Cervit, an alloy people still turn up now and then but nobody knows how to make any more. By the time the crates were empty we had around ninety kilos of it, white and black, and we split it between us.

The Orb Room

↑ ladder

A ladder led up into a room of sturdy metal tables, with a broken half-dome of panels hanging from the ceiling. Along one wall, four crystal orbs sat on pedestals, each a third bigger than a football and full of strange, mutated underground plants. Our skin prickled as we came close. So did Drivik's tongue, because of course he licked one. Detect magic showed nothing, Vicaster's arcana could only offer “alchemy,” and the machine overhead hung just too high for Muradin to get a proper look at.

The Fabric Room

↓ down the chain

From there Drivik simply stepped onto the chain and slid down it, trusting to Reliable Talent, into a room buried in heaps of old fabric. The rest of us climbed down after him, more carefully. Further in, a ladder dropped away again, and below it something was glowing a faint green.

We would come back through here later and find out what the heaps were hiding: spectres wrapped in chains, rising out of the cloth. It was a short fight, and once beaten they fled.

The Poison Cage

↓ ladder

The green came from a glowing ball inside a metal cage. Vicaster sent his familiar through the bars in sphinx of wonder form, and it died, painfully. He summoned it back and gave it candy, which seemed only fair. Getting close himself cost him 22 poison damage, and when Drivik put an arrow into the ball, the arrow simply vanished.

Vicaster's arcana supplied the rest: this was a portal to the para-elemental plane of poison. We gave it a very wide berth on the way past.

The Crystal Hall

↓ ladder, 20 ft

Twenty feet further down, the ladder opened onto a room made of crystal, spirals carved into its floor. Six women of crystal lounged on platforms of salt — Drivik would later lick one to be sure — with spikes growing from their bodies and broken gemstones where their eyes should be. Drivik took one look and scrambled back up.

The familiar went down in his place and asked how they were doing. They seemed amused, and allowed that we might not be a treat if we minded our manners. So Vicaster went down first, with a bow, and the rest of us followed.

They spoke one at a time, each sister taking her turn. They had been brought here against their will, from a place where drought and purification rule, and had stayed this long — though time, they said, is relative — because one of them had had her heart stolen. That heart turned out to be the milky crystal from chest 19 of the vault. We gave it back, they healed their sister, and then they told us where we were.

We are in the Cradle.

The place we had been looking for all along. A facility built to study deadly and dangerous things, they said, with an observatory at its very bottom where the builders searched for whatever their research needed.
